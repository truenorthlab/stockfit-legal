Coupon bond

A bond paying interest regularly until it matures

In detail

A coupon bond pays interest at set intervals while you hold it and returns the face value at maturity. Most government and corporate bonds work this way, often paying twice a year. Receiving income along the way suits anyone who needs cash before maturity. A discount bond does the opposite, paying nothing until the gap at maturity.

Example

A $1 million bond at 1% a year pays $5,000 every six months.
