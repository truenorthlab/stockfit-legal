Record date

The day that fixes who receives the dividend

In detail

The record date is the day the shareholder register decides who gets the dividend. Because settlement takes a few days, you must actually buy that many business days earlier. The day after that cut-off is the ex-dividend date, when the price tends to drop by roughly the dividend.

Example

On a stock paying $30, the price typically opens about $30 lower on the ex-dividend date. Buy after the cut-off and you miss that payment.

Differences by country

Japan — In Japan settlement takes two business days, so the last day to buy is two business days before the record date. Shareholder perks are fixed on the same date.

South Korea — In Korea settlement takes two business days, so a purchase must be executed two business days before the record date to receive the dividend (as of 2026). Record dates differ by company, so check the filing.
